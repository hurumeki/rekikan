# Learning Design Principles

> Source: `rekikan-concept.md` — Appendix

---

The learning design of this app is based on the following principles.

**"Big-to-small" zoom-in learning:** An approach known as "schema construction" in cognitive science. By first building a broad framework in the learner's mind and then attaching details to it, the result is structured understanding rather than disconnected pieces of knowledge.

**Description-first introduction:** Before terms and dates, learners experience the "atmosphere" and "image of change" of each era. Since it requires zero terminological knowledge, it functions as an entry point for beginners.

**Experiential learning through sorting:** Rather than memorizing dates, learners physically acquire a sense of "roughly around this period." Even without knowing exact dates, having a sense of order provides a foundation for understanding historical cause-and-effect.

**Category recognition through visual tags:** When learning terms, visual tags link "what kind of knowledge this is" to the term. This helps distinguish easily confused terms and contributes to organizing memory.

**Scaffolding through hints:** Beginners work with hints ON and turn them OFF as they gain confidence. The same stage can be attempted at self-adjusted difficulty, maintaining motivation for repeated study.

**Multi-perspective understanding through cross-regional learning:** By crossing Japanese and world history, learners develop a sense of contemporaneity — such as "while it was the Kamakura Period (鎌倉時代) in Japan, the Crusades were happening in Europe" — leading to historical understanding that transcends the framework of a single nation's history.
