# Product Overview

> Source: `rekikan-concept.md` — Sections 1.1–1.5

---

## 1.1 Product Name

"Rekikan" (れきかん — Grasping the Sense of History)

## 1.2 Concept

A puzzle game where players arrange history cards in chronological order. Starting from broad historical periods and progressively moving into finer divisions, this learning app helps users naturally understand the flow of history.

## 1.3 Background and Problem Statement

History learning faces the following challenges:

- Approaches that begin with memorizing individual events and dates set a high barrier for beginners, making it difficult to grasp the big picture
- Existing history card games (the "arrange by date" type) are designed for people who already have a certain level of knowledge, making them too difficult for children and beginners
- It is more effective for knowledge retention to first grasp the "general flow" by region and era before moving into details
- Many terms are easily confused despite belonging to different eras — for example, the Jōei Code (御成敗式目, じょうえいしきもく) and the Laws for Military Houses (武家諸法度, ぶけしょはっと). One cause is that learners memorize terms without a clear sense of "what category of knowledge this belongs to"

## 1.4 Solution Approach

**Realize "big-to-small" zoom-in learning through a hierarchical structure.**

- First, without relying on terminology, arrange description cards that explain the "feel of each era" to build an image of the historical flow
- Then, progressively move into finer divisions using era names and term cards
- Attach visual tags for region, era band, and category to each card, enabling intuitive recognition of "what kind of knowledge this is"
- Clearing a broader division unlocks finer divisions, guiding learning order while providing motivation to advance

## 1.5 Target Users

The app serves a wide range of users:

- Upper elementary to middle school students (preview and review for history classes)
- High school students (building a foundation for exam preparation)
- Adults revisiting history (those who want to organize history as general knowledge)
